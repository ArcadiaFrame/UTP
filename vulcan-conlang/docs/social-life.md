# Social life, survival, bonds and rank

Batch 21 adds the vocabulary of living among others in a hard land: how Vulcans receive a guest and keep a reputation, how they confide and court, how a desert people stays alive, and how the war-Houses and the training halls rank their members. Batch 22 adds life inside a bond: what the bond feels, comfort, quarrels and their mending, and parting. Most words are built from older roots, because these are social and institutional meanings (see `word-building.md`). Every form below renders from the dictionary.

## The heart-whisper

*kekru-vrakh* (verb) and *kekru-vrakhuth* (noun), "heart-whisper," name one act: confiding a matter of the heart about someone who is not present. It covers telling a t'hy'la "I am drawn to T'Lara," telling a friend "she is drawn to you," and weighing a possible courtship with family before anyone approaches.

- **Who may hear it.** Only bond-kin: t'hy'la, bondmates, siblings. The same whispered sentence told to anyone else is *vrakhuth*, rumor ("a whispering"). The two words share a root on purpose: the difference is the listener.
- **The one who keeps it** is the *kekru-sar-veh*, "heart-keeper." To keep a confidence is *shofoo-sar*.
- **The declaration** made to the person desired is a different act: *kekru-kauk*, "heart-tell."
- **Standard grammar keeps its discipline even here.** The absent one's feeling is never stated bare: *kahol tok ish-veh aukh*, "there is attraction in her, I conclude." A Standard Vulcan in a heart-whisper still says how they know.

## Life in the bond

Batch 22 adds the vocabulary of life between bondmates and t'hy'la. Two facts shape it. Vulcans are touch-telepaths, so closeness is described as what the mind and the bond feel. And Standard reserve still holds in private, so most of these words let a feeling be reported as evidence rather than displayed.

**What the bond reports.** These three words are statements of fact, so they take *tekh*, "known through touch or the bond":

| Vulcan | English |
|---|---|
| *koon-eekhikh* | bond-warmth: the other's presence felt through the bond |
| *koon-kisuhk* | bond-ache: the pain of a long distance |
| *koon-kut* | bond-silence: the stillness where a bonded mind is no longer felt, through great distance or death |

A Standard Vulcan who would never say "I missed you" can say *koon-kut silukh*, "the bond-silence was long," and has said it. The frank word exists too: *torrir-nal*, "miss" ("feel the distance"), with its noun *torrir-naluth*. Said in Standard, it is still named, not displayed: *torrir-naluth tok nash-veh kon*.

## Social life

Hospitality in the desert begins with water. The *ekas-vauksakh* is the cup of water offered to a guest before any word is spoken; a guest who is not offered it has been insulted.
