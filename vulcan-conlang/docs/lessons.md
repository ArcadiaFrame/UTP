# Learning Vulcan: lessons

A course in Federation Standard Vulcan for writers and learners. Each lesson gives new words, one grammar point, examples, and practice with answers. Every Vulcan word renders from the dictionary. Pronunciation is in `pronunciation.md` and the full rules are in `grammar.md`.

---

## Lesson 1: Greetings and courtesy

**Words**

| Vulcan | English |
|---|---|
| *t'nar* | greetings (formal opening) |
| *jaral* | likewise (the reply) |
| *dif-tor he smusma* | live long and prosper |
| *sochya* eh *dif* | peace and long life (the reply) |
| *ev* | welcome |
| *ook* | farewell |
| *chaya* | thank you |
| *pok'* | pardon, excuse me |
| *il* / *sa* | yes / no |
| *kor* | please (before a request) |

**Grammar: please comes first.** *kor* opens a request, and the verb comes last. *Kor kir pokok.* "Please say it again."

**Examples**

- *T'nar pahk sarat y'rani.* "Greetings; I extend regard to your house." Reply: *T'nar jaral.*
- *Dif-tor he smusma.* Reply: *Sochya eh dif.*
- *Il, chaya.* "Yes, thank you." · *Sa, chaya.* "No, thank you."

**Practice.** Say in Vulcan: (1) Welcome. (2) Thank you. (3) No, thank you. (4) Please say it again.

**Answers.** (1) *Ev.* (2) *Chaya.* (3) *Sa, chaya.* (4) *Kor kir pokok.*

## Lesson 2: I am, this is

**Words**

| Vulcan | English |
|---|---|
| *nash-veh* | I ("this one") |
| *tu* | you |
| *ish-veh* | he, she ("that one") |
| *nash* / *ish* | this / that |
| *wim* | be (identity) |
| *gor* | name |
| *keru* / *sau* | my / your |
| *ukhiv* / *wonul* | good / bad |
| *t'omyan* / *hik'ak* | big / small |
| *mooli* / *kaun* | old / new |
| *fukhin* | house |
| *vo-veh* | healer |

**Grammar.**

- **No verb "to be" in descriptions.** *Fukhin mooli.* "The house is old."
- **Identity uses *wim*,** and canon adds *-ish* for "I": *Saavik wimish*, "I am Saavik."
- **The noun comes first,** then the adjective, then the possessor: *fukhin mooli keru*, "my old house."

**Examples**

- *Gor keru Sorel.* "My name is Sorel." · *Vo-veh wimish.* "I am a healer."
- *Fukhin sau t'omyan.* "Your house is big." · *Nash ukhiv.* "This is good."

**Practice.** (1) The house is new. (2) My house is small. (3) This is bad. (4) I am a healer.

**Answers.** (1) *Fukhin kaun.* (2) *Fukhin keru hik'ak.* (3) *Nash wonul.* (4) *Vo-veh wimish.*

## Lesson 3: Doing things

**Words**

| Vulcan | English |
|---|---|
| *kosha* | see |
| *tu'o* | know |
| *dawru* | want |
| *sarlah* / *lish* | come / go |
| *sokzha* / *makha* | eat / drink |
| *ekas* / *kul* | water / food |
| *shelek* | ship |
| *tan* | here |
| *tir* / *va* | now / tomorrow |

**Grammar: subject, object, verb.**

- The verb comes last, and the object takes *-wizh*.
- **Tense:** *-pil* means "is doing," *-un* means past, and *dungi* before the verb means future.

**Examples**

- *Nash-veh shelek-wizh kosha.* "I see the ship."
- *Nash-veh ekas makha-pil.* "I am drinking water."
- *Ish-veh tanfi sarlah-un.* "He came here."
- *Va tu dungi lish.* "Tomorrow you will go."

**Practice.** (1) I want food. (2) You saw the ship. (3) He is eating. (4) Tomorrow I will come.

**Answers.** (1) *Nash-veh kul dawru.* (2) *Tu shelek-wizh kosha-un.* (3) *Ish-veh sokzha-pil.* (4) *Va nash-veh dungi sarlah.*

## Lesson 4: Asking, and saying how you know

**Words**

| Vulcan | English |
|---|---|
| *wakli* / *thuh* / *fa* / *tun* / *ghishun* | what / who / where / when / why |
| *ar-ok'* | how many |
| *kuth* | yes-or-no question marker |
| *ho* | I saw it |
| *aukh* | I conclude it |
| *k'en* | I was told it |
| *haska* | understand |

**Grammar.**

- **Question words go first.** For a yes-or-no question, open with *kuth*.
- **Answer by repeating the verb.**
- **An evidential ends the sentence** when you need to say how you know. Vulcans use them far more than English speakers say "apparently."

**Examples**

- *Fa tu lish-pil?* "Where are you going?" · *Wakli gor sau?* "What is your name?"
- *Kuth tu haska?* "Do you understand?" Answer: *Haska.* "(I) understand."
- *Shelek tanfi sarlah-un ho.* "The ship came here. (I saw it.)"
- *Shelek tanfi sarlah-un aukh.* "The ship came here. (I conclude.)"

**Practice.** (1) Who is he? (2) Do you want water? (3) He came. (I was told.) (4) How many ships?

**Answers.** (1) *Thuh ish-veh?* (2) *Kuth tu ekas dawru?* (3) *Ish-veh sarlah-un k'en.* (4) *Ar-ok' shelek-k'oo?*

## Lesson 5: Describing, comparing, counting

**Words**

| Vulcan | English |
|---|---|
| *sinish* / *feiri* | hot / cold |
| *ahet* / *yeikuf* | fast / slow |
| *t'ei* | than |
| *li* / *osh* | more / less |
| *nit* | most |
| *vak*, *vak-vauk*, *yik-vak*, *pizh* | ten, eleven, twenty, a hundred |

**Grammar.**

- **Comparison** is X, then Y *t'ei*, then *li* or *osh*, then the quality.
- **Numbers** follow the noun and are regular: ten-one is eleven, two-ten is twenty.

**Examples**

- *Vran pelnuhl t'ei li sinish.* "The sun is hotter than the sister world."
- *Shelek-k'oo yik-vak.* "Twenty ships." · *nit ahet*, "fastest."

**Practice.** (1) This is colder than that. (2) Eleven ships. (3) The slowest. (4) Thirty days.

**Answers.** (1) *Nash ish t'ei li feiri.* (2) *Shelek-k'oo vak-vauk.* (3) *Nit yeikuf.* (4) *Tanakh-k'oo veet-vak.*

## Lesson 6: Building words

**Grammar.** Learn the pieces, and the vocabulary multiplies:

| Piece | Means |
|---|---|
| *-veh* | person |
| *-puhl* | place |
| *-uth* | the act |
| *-hol* | the quality |
| *-uk* | having, -y |
| *-tor* | make, do |
| *uhf-* | without |
| *kir-* | again |
| *-kam* | little |

**Examples.** From *haumok* "learn":

- *haumok-veh*, the learning one: a student
- *haumokpuhl*, the learning place: a school
- *haumokuth*, the act: learning

From *vo* "heal":

- *vo-veh*, a healer
- *vopuhl*, a hospital
- *vouth*, healing

**Practice.** Work out the meanings: (1) *sharshei-veh* (2) *voroorpuhl* (3) *uhf-kupuk* (4) *kir-sarlah* (5) *sheka-kam* (6) *ootakhol*

**Answers.** (1) teaching one: teacher. (2) sleeping place: bedroom. (3) without possible: impossible. (4) again-come: return. (5) little stone: pebble. (6) free-ness: freedom.

## Lesson 7: Speaking up, speaking down

**Words**

| Vulcan | English |
|---|---|
| *cheil* | respected… (before a title) |
| *ihk-veh* | you, honored one |
| *-mush* / *-fum* | honorific / humble ending |
| *suh* | formal sentence ending |
| *t'hy'la* | t'hy'la (no honorifics at all) |

**Grammar.**

- **Raise the other person** with *-mush* on the verb or with *ihk-veh*.
- **Lower yourself** with *-fum*, and call yourself *nash-veh* ("this one").
- **Between t'hy'la, use none of it.**

**Examples**

- *Cheil ovas, kor tu stariben-mush.* "Respected teacher, please speak."
- *Nash-veh suhk tu'o-fum.* "This one does not know."
- *Tu t'hy'la keru.* "You are my t'hy'la." (Plain speech is itself the honor.)

**Practice.** (1) Respected healer, please come. (2) This one does not understand. (3) Make (1) formal.

**Answers.** (1) *Cheil vo-veh, kor sarlah.* (2) *Nash-veh suhk haska-fum.* (3) *Cheil vo-veh, kor sarlah suh.*

## Lesson 8: Feeling and touch, the Vulcan way

**Words**

| Vulcan | English |
|---|---|
| *sik*, *neri*, *kekal*, *mikshak* | anger, fear, grief, joy |
| *kon* | noted (closes an emotion statement) |
| *tekh* | known by touch |
| *usul* | touch, asking leave |
| *seekak* | you have leave |
| *suthnoo-wizh ral* | forgive the display |

**Grammar.**

- **A Standard Vulcan does not say "I am afraid."** They say "fear is in me. Noted."
- **They do not state someone else's feeling** without an evidential.
- **They ask leave before any touch.**

**Examples**

- *Neri tok nash-veh kon.* "There is fear in me. Noted."
- *Ish-veh kekal aukh.* "She is grieving, I conclude."
- *Kor nash-veh usul?* "May this one touch you?" Reply: *Seekak.*
- *Kor suthnoo-wizh ral.* "Please forgive the display."

**Romulan contrast:** a Romulan states feeling freely and marks rank instead. *Mnek'nra* is "good," said up to a superior.

**Practice.** (1) There is joy in me. Noted. (2) He is angry, I conclude. (3) May this one touch you?

**Answers.** (1) *Mikshak tok nash-veh kon.* (2) *Ish-veh sik aukh.* (3) *Kor nash-veh usul?*
