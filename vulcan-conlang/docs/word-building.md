# Word-building

Vulcan has a small stock of old roots and builds everything else from them. A student is a "learning one" (*haumok-veh*), a school a "learning place" (*haumokpuhl*), and the Federation is "worlds joined" (*spak-kau*), on the pattern of canon *kash-kau*, "minds joined." The dictionary has 3,688 entries: 211 canon words, 1,278 roots, 2125 words built from older ones, 72 designed words and 2 loan; 99 of the entries are Romulan only.

## Where the rules come from

1. **Canon Vulcan is the base.** Every process below is modeled on something canon shows:
   - the bound verb *-tor*, "do, make," in *dif-tor*, "live long"
   - the bound noun *ve*, "one, person," in *ish-ve* and *nash-ve*
   - *kau*, "joined," in *kash-kau*
   - the participle *-ik* in *seheik*, "declared"
   - the diminutive *-kam*
   - the old compounds *plak tow* (blood fever), *pon far* (time of fire) and *koon-ut* (bond rite)
2. **Anthropology and evolutionary linguistics decide what is old.** The meanings every human society names first are roots, and so are the Vulcan ones: body, kin, desert and sky, motion, eating, the basic emotions, small numbers, and the basic qualities (these are the meanings on the Swadesh and Leipzig-Jakarta lists). Meanings that come with social complexity, institutions, abstraction and technology are built later from older words. Colour terms arrive in the Berlin-Kay order.
3. **English, Latin and Japanese cross-check.** Where two of the three build a meaning from another word, Vulcan does too, but with its own pieces. Latin *veri-ficare*, "make true," sits behind Vulcan *patu-tor*.

**Prefixes grew out of prepositions**, the way canon *fi'* "on, toward" became the ending *-fi*:

- *uhf-* "without, un-": *uhf-kupuk* impossible, *uhf-nawak* infinity, *uhf-laka* atom ("uncuttable")
- *kir-* "again, re-": *kir-sarlah* return, *kir-tu'o* recognize ("know again")
- *pu-* "before, pre-": *pu-pokok* predict ("say before"), *pu-tor* prepare
- *tok-* "in": *tok-srur* include, *tok-koshauth* insight
- *tol-* "out": *tol-vurik* express ("push out")
- *er-* "with": *er-vushhush* compassion ("suffering with")

**Fields of study** follow the pattern *mik t'X*, "inquiry of X," as in *mik t'wul* science. For example *mik t'mune* (astronomy, "inquiry of stars"), *mik t'suni* (biology) and *mik t'chak* (history, "inquiry of the past").

**Two roots joined** make compounds, the oldest process of all: *pur-kato* capital ("head-city"), *vran-skur* east ("sun-rise"), *seli-ekas* mirage ("false water"), *far-volkis* firepot (canon *far*, "fire," + pot).

## Order of the pieces

The order is fixed, as in Japanese. Prefix, then root, then building endings, then honorific or humble, then *-pil* (ongoing), then *-un* (past). *-wizh* goes on the last word of the object phrase, and the evidential comes last in the sentence.

*kir-sarlah-pil-un*, "was returning" · *haumok-veh-k'oo-wizh*, "the students" (as object)

## The era rule

A built word can never be older than its pieces; the checker refuses one that is (E11). So "green," *plakuk*, built on canon *plak* "blood," is a Classical word, not a Tribal one. Quality nouns in *-hol* become productive in the Classical era, and Surak's philosophers multiplied them.

## Where Vulcan culture overrides

- **Green is blood-coloured** (*plakuk*), because Vulcan blood is green.
- **Blue is void-coloured** (*zhokouk*). Vulcan's sky is pale and red, so blue is rare and late, the colour of the deep sky at dusk.
- **A mirage is "false water"** (*seli-ekas*), a desert people's word.
- **Privacy is "the self kept to itself"** (*luukhol*): self + adjective + quality.
- **The arts are Ways**, as in Japanese: calligraphy is *shlil-motol*, "the way of writing."

## Making a new word

1. Search the dictionary first.
2. If a basic meaning is missing (a body part, a desert animal), it gets a new root from the generator.
3. Otherwise build it from existing pieces with the endings above, keeping the era rule.
4. Add it to `lexicon/meanings.csv` (a root) or `tools/derivation.py` (a built word), then run the build. The checker refuses duplicates and sound-alikes.

## Numbers and kin

**Numbers are regular**, as in Japanese: ten-one is eleven and two-ten is twenty. A number follows its noun, as other modifiers do.

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 *vauk* | 10 *vak* | 11 *vak-vauk* | 20 *yik-vak* |
| 12 *vak-yik* | 19 *vak-ke* | 30 *veet-vak* | 90 *ke-vak* |
| 100 *pizh* | 1,000 *te* | first *vauksakh* | second *yiksakh* |
| a third *veet-khasni* | a quarter *ir-khasni* | a tenth *vak-khasni* | a fraction *khasni-seekher* |
| once *vauk-pon* | twice *yik-pon* | one by one *vauk-vauk* | a trio *veetot* |
| hundredth *pizhsakh* | decimal point *vak-kiruk* | about ten *vak weellirfu* | exactly *srerfu* |

**How often, how many each, how many together.** "Times" is *pon* "time": *vauk-pon* once, *veet-pon* three times. A
number said twice is distributive, as in Turkish and Japanese: *vauk-vauk* one each, one by one; *yik-yik* two by two.
A number with *-ot* names a set: *veetot* a trio, *irot* a foursome (a pair stays *ezh*, a dozen *eiv*).

**Arithmetic.** *moluk* add, *srur-wa* subtract ("take away"), *ok'-tor* multiply ("make many"), *yuk* divide;
*sakatuth* remainder; *ezh-seekher* even number ("pair-number"), *uhf-ezh-seekher* odd, *uhf-yuk-seekher* prime
("undivided number"); *yik-tulmee* square ("second power"), *yik-ishuk* square root.

**Two counts.** Daily life and science use the ten-count, *vak-shil*. Temples, calendars and bond rites keep the
seven-count, *huk-shil*, after the roughly seven-year cycle: its unit is *hukot* "a seven", so ten is
*vauk hukot veet* "one seven, three", and forty-nine is *huk-hukot*, the great seven, the full ritual count. A span of
seven years is a *huk-ka'ke*, and *hukuk* "sevenfold" means, in rites, complete. (*Huk-huk* would be the distributive
"seven each", so the great seven is built on *hukot*.)

**Kin terms are descriptive**, as in clan societies. An aunt or uncle is "parent-sibling," *zheenak-khano*, as Swedish says *farbror*, "father-brother." A niece or nephew is "sibling-child," *khano-wan*. A cousin is a "clan-sibling," *ketol-khano*: in a society of great Houses, cousins grow up as siblings, as in the Hawaiian and Iroquois kin systems. Elder and younger siblings keep their own old roots (*alen*, *tasas*), because seniority matters.
