# After Surak: the contemplative culture, its sayings, and the surveillance years

Surak's reform did more than suppress emotion. It built a culture of practice, as Zen and Confucianism did in Japan and China: a set of disciplines for the mind, and a code of conduct for the household and the state. Its words are mostly built from older roots, because a reform names new ideals with familiar words. This document covers that vocabulary, the sayings it produced, and its dark side: the paranoid state of the 22nd-century High Command, which lasted until the Kir'Shara (2154) and the founding of the Federation (2161).

What is canon: the Teachings of Surak, *kaiidth* "what is, is," Tuvok's meditation lamp, the V'Shar, the High Command, its persecution of the Syrrannites and of melders, and the Kir'Shara. Everything else is design. The Earth analogues are noted because the design brief asked for them, but each word is built from Vulcan roots and Vulcan reasons.

Sayings are terse: no evidential and no politeness particle, because a saying belongs to no speaker. They are kept in `corpus/idioms.csv` and in the Phrasebook.

## The surveillance years (22nd century, to 2161)

Under the High Command (*fusha-harikuth*), logic hardened into orthodoxy (*srer-nahp*, "correct-thought"). The V'Shar (*v'shar*) watched, melders (*luhl-veh*) were hunted as deviants, and the Syrrannites were driven into the Forge. The language of the time shows it.

**The sanctioned-speech particle.** Bureaucratic speech of the period ends an approved statement with *lul*, "this may be said; this is the approved account." A careful speaker used it to mark a remark as safe to repeat, and so its absence marked a remark as dangerous. After the Kir'Shara and the founding of the Federation it fell out of use. Today it is archaic, heard in histories, or in irony, as when a Vulcan officer gives a press statement and a friend adds *lul*. The Romulan discretion particle *hwil* does the opposite job: it marks what may *not* be repeated. Both peoples, cut off from each other, grew a grammar of secrecy.

**After 2161.** The Federation-era answer is *kesehol*, speaking freely, and *gor-srer-tor* returns to its first meaning: precision, not orthodoxy.

## Using this in fiction

- **A meditation scene:** the master speaks in sayings and imperatives; the student names feelings with *kon*. See the Phrasebook scene "the meditation hall."
- **A 2150s scene:** officials end statements with *lul*; ordinary people speak little and say *tuvri-k'oo p'eren-k'oo paka*, "the walls have ears." See "a checkpoint, 2150s."
- **The 23rd and 24th centuries:** the surveillance words are history. A Vulcan who uses *lul* sincerely sounds like a relic of the High Command.
