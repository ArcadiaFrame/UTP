# Pronunciation guide

How to read Vulcan and Romulan aloud. The spelling is consistent: each letter or letter pair always stands for the same sound.

## Vowels

| Spelling | Sound | Like | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | /a/ | f**a**ther (short) | *{day}* "day" |
| e | /e/ | b**e**d | *{ve}* "one, person", *{shade}* "shade" |
| i | /i/ | mach**i**ne (short) | *{dif}* "long life", *{isik}* "coin" |
| o | /o/ | **o**r | *{tor}* "do, make", *{pon}* "time" |
| u | /u/ | r**u**le (short) | *{tu}* "you", *{kup}* "can" |
| uh | /ə/ | **a**bout | *{not}* "not", *{pain}* "pain" |
| aa, ee, oo | long /aː iː uː/ | the vowel held | *{plomeek}*, *{koon}* "bond" |
| y between consonants | /ɨ/ | a short "i" said far back | *t'hy'la* |
| ai, au, ei, oi | diphthongs | **eye**, c**ow**, d**ay**, b**oy** | *{these}* "these", *{kau}* "joined", *{teach}* "teach", *{kroika}* "enough!" |

Canon often writes a vowel plus *h* (*sahrafel*, *kroykah*). The *h* is an English spelling habit; the standard spelling drops it (*sarafel*, *kroika*).

## Consonants

| Spelling | Sound | Like |
|---|---|---|
| kh | /x/ | Scottish lo**ch**, German Ba**ch** |
| sh | /ʃ/ | **sh**ip |
| zh | /ʒ/ | mea**s**ure |
| th | /θ/ | **th**in |
| ch | /tʃ/ | **ch**urch |
| j | /dʒ/ | **j**ump |
| ' (apostrophe) | glottal stop /ʔ/ | the catch in uh-**'**oh |
| r | tapped or rolled | Spanish *pero* |
| lh | /ɬ/ (Old Vulcan only) | Welsh **ll** in *Llanelli*; Standard Vulcan has *l* |
| q | /q/ (Old Vulcan only) | a *k* far back in the throat; Standard Vulcan has *k* |

Other letters sound as in English. A doubled consonant is held longer.

## Stress

Stress falls on the **first syllable of the root**; prefixes and endings are unstressed. A compound stresses the first syllable of each member, the second most strongly. This matches the canon phonetic scripts *KAH-trah* and *PLAK-toe-fahl*.

*{school}*: HAU-mok-puhl · *{student}*: HAU-mok-VE · *{federation}*: SPAK-KAU

## By era

- **Old Vulcan and Ceremonial** are harsh: ejective stops (a popped *k'*, *t'*, *p'*), the uvular *q*, the Welsh-like *lh*, and nasal clusters (*nt*, *mp*). Use them for the Gol liturgy and ancient rites.
- **Standard Vulcan** has smoothed these sounds by the 2240s. *q* becomes *k*, *lh* becomes *l*, and the ejectives become glottal stops.
- **Romulan** turns the ejectives into the breathy *kh*, *th* and *fv*, rolls a doubled *rr*, and breaks vowels into the glides *ae* and *oa*. *hw* is a breathy *wh*, as in older English *which*, and *ll* is the Welsh sound. Duane's *mnhei'sahe* is said MNHAY-sah-heh.

## Reading practice

| Vulcan | Said | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *{dif-tor e smusma}* | DIF-tor heh SMOOS-mah | live long and prosper |
| *{t'hy'la}* | t-HƗ-lah, with a glottal catch after the t | t'hy'la |
| *{anger} {in} {nash-ve} {noting}* | SIK tok NASH-veh KON | There is anger in me. Noted. |
| *{healer}* | VO-veh | healer |
| *{plak tow}* | PLAK TOH | blood fever |
