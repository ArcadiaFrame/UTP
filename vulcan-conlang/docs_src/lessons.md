# Learning Vulcan: lessons

A course in Federation Standard Vulcan for writers and learners. Each lesson gives new words, one grammar point, examples, and practice with answers. Every Vulcan word renders from the dictionary. Pronunciation is in `pronunciation.md` and the full rules are in `grammar.md`.

---

## Lesson 1: Greetings and courtesy

**Words**

| Vulcan | English |
|---|---|
| *{t'nar}* | greetings (formal opening) |
| *{jaral}* | likewise (the reply) |
| *{dif-tor e smusma}* | live long and prosper |
| *{sochya}* eh *{dif}* | peace and long life (the reply) |
| *{welcome}* | welcome |
| *{farewell}* | farewell |
| *{thanks}* | thank you |
| *{pardon}* | pardon, excuse me |
| *{yes}* / *{no}* | yes / no |
| *{request}* | please (before a request) |

**Grammar: please comes first.** *{request}* opens a request, and the verb comes last. *{request} {again} {say}.* "Please say it again."

**Examples**

- *{t'nar} {pahk} {sarat} {y'rani}.* "Greetings; I extend regard to your house." Reply: *{t'nar} {jaral}.*
- *{dif-tor e smusma}.* Reply: *{sochya} eh {dif}.*
- *{yes}, {thanks}.* "Yes, thank you." · *{no}, {thanks}.* "No, thank you."

**Practice.** Say in Vulcan: (1) Welcome. (2) Thank you. (3) No, thank you. (4) Please say it again.

**Answers.** (1) *{welcome}.* (2) *{thanks}.* (3) *{no}, {thanks}.* (4) *{request} {again} {say}.*

## Lesson 2: I am, this is

**Words**

| Vulcan | English |
|---|---|
| *{nash-ve}* | I ("this one") |
| *{tu}* | you |
| *{ish-ve}* | he, she ("that one") |
| *{nash}* / *{ish}* | this / that |
| *{be}* | be (identity) |
| *{name}* | name |
| *{my}* / *{your}* | my / your |
| *{good}* / *{bad}* | good / bad |
| *{big}* / *{small}* | big / small |
| *{old}* / *{new}* | old / new |
| *{house}* | house |
| *{healer}* | healer |

**Grammar.**

- **No verb "to be" in descriptions.** *{house} {old}.* "The house is old."
- **Identity uses *{be}*,** and canon adds *-ish* for "I": *Saavik wimish*, "I am Saavik."
- **The noun comes first,** then the adjective, then the possessor: *{house} {old} {my}*, "my old house."

**Examples**

- *{name} {my} Sorel.* "My name is Sorel." · *{healer} {be}ish.* "I am a healer."
- *{house} {your} {big}.* "Your house is big." · *{nash} {good}.* "This is good."

**Practice.** (1) The house is new. (2) My house is small. (3) This is bad. (4) I am a healer.

**Answers.** (1) *{house} {new}.* (2) *{house} {my} {small}.* (3) *{nash} {bad}.* (4) *{healer} {be}ish.*

## Lesson 3: Doing things

**Words**

| Vulcan | English |
|---|---|
| *{see}* | see |
| *{know}* | know |
| *{want}* | want |
| *{come}* / *{go}* | come / go |
| *{eat}* / *{drink}* | eat / drink |
| *{water}* / *{food}* | water / food |
| *{ship}* | ship |
| *{here}* | here |
| *{now}* / *{tomorrow}* | now / tomorrow |

**Grammar: subject, object, verb.**

- The verb comes last, and the object takes *-{object-wizh}*.
- **Tense:** *-{ongoing}* means "is doing," *-{past-un}* means past, and *{dungi}* before the verb means future.

**Examples**

- *{nash-ve} {ship}-{object-wizh} {see}.* "I see the ship."
- *{nash-ve} {water} {drink}-{ongoing}.* "I am drinking water."
- *{ish-ve} {here}{toward} {come}-{past-un}.* "He came here."
- *{tomorrow} {tu} {dungi} {go}.* "Tomorrow you will go."

**Practice.** (1) I want food. (2) You saw the ship. (3) He is eating. (4) Tomorrow I will come.

**Answers.** (1) *{nash-ve} {food} {want}.* (2) *{tu} {ship}-{object-wizh} {see}-{past-un}.* (3) *{ish-ve} {eat}-{ongoing}.* (4) *{tomorrow} {nash-ve} {dungi} {come}.*

## Lesson 4: Asking, and saying how you know

**Words**

| Vulcan | English |
|---|---|
| *{what}* / *{who}* / *{where}* / *{when}* / *{gishun}* | what / who / where / when / why |
| *{how-many}* | how many |
| *{question}* | yes-or-no question marker |
| *{ev-seen}* | I saw it |
| *{ev-inferred}* | I conclude it |
| *{ev-reported}* | I was told it |
| *{understand}* | understand |

**Grammar.**

- **Question words go first.** For a yes-or-no question, open with *{question}*.
- **Answer by repeating the verb.**
- **An evidential ends the sentence** when you need to say how you know. Vulcans use them far more than English speakers say "apparently."

**Examples**

- *{where} {tu} {go}-{ongoing}?* "Where are you going?" · *{what} {name} {your}?* "What is your name?"
- *{question} {tu} {understand}?* "Do you understand?" Answer: *{understand}.* "(I) understand."
- *{ship} {here}{toward} {come}-{past-un} {ev-seen}.* "The ship came here. (I saw it.)"
- *{ship} {here}{toward} {come}-{past-un} {ev-inferred}.* "The ship came here. (I conclude.)"

**Practice.** (1) Who is he? (2) Do you want water? (3) He came. (I was told.) (4) How many ships?

**Answers.** (1) *{who} {ish-ve}?* (2) *{question} {tu} {water} {want}?* (3) *{ish-ve} {come}-{past-un} {ev-reported}.* (4) *{how-many} {ship}-{plural}?*

## Lesson 5: Describing, comparing, counting

**Words**

| Vulcan | English |
|---|---|
| *{hot}* / *{cold}* | hot / cold |
| *{fast}* / *{slow}* | fast / slow |
| *{than}* | than |
| *{more}* / *{less}* | more / less |
| *{most}* | most |
| *{ten}*, *{eleven}*, *{twenty}*, *{hundred}* | ten, eleven, twenty, a hundred |

**Grammar.**

- **Comparison** is X, then Y *{than}*, then *{more}* or *{less}*, then the quality.
- **Numbers** follow the noun and are regular: ten-one is eleven, two-ten is twenty.

**Examples**

- *{sun} {sister-world} {than} {more} {hot}.* "The sun is hotter than the sister world."
- *{ship}-{plural} {twenty}.* "Twenty ships." · *{most} {fast}*, "fastest."

**Practice.** (1) This is colder than that. (2) Eleven ships. (3) The slowest. (4) Thirty days.

**Answers.** (1) *{nash} {ish} {than} {more} {cold}.* (2) *{ship}-{plural} {eleven}.* (3) *{most} {slow}.* (4) *{day}-{plural} {thirty}.*

## Lesson 6: Building words

**Grammar.** Learn the pieces, and the vocabulary multiplies:

| Piece | Means |
|---|---|
| *-{ve}* | person |
| *-{place-suf}* | place |
| *-{action}* | the act |
| *-{abstract}* | the quality |
| *-{adjectival}* | having, -y |
| *-{tor}* | make, do |
| *{without}-* | without |
| *{again}-* | again |
| *-{kam}* | little |

**Examples.** From *{learn}* "learn":

- *{student}*, the learning one: a student
- *{school}*, the learning place: a school
- *{learning}*, the act: learning

From *{vo}* "heal":

- *{healer}*, a healer
- *{hospital}*, a hospital
- *{healing}*, healing

**Practice.** Work out the meanings: (1) *{teacher}* (2) *{bedroom}* (3) *{impossible}* (4) *{return}* (5) *{pebble}* (6) *{freedom}*

**Answers.** (1) teaching one: teacher. (2) sleeping place: bedroom. (3) without possible: impossible. (4) again-come: return. (5) little stone: pebble. (6) free-ness: freedom.

## Lesson 7: Speaking up, speaking down

**Words**

| Vulcan | English |
|---|---|
| *{vocative}* | respected… (before a title) |
| *{you-honored}* | you, honored one |
| *-{honorific}* / *-{humble}* | honorific / humble ending |
| *{formal}* | formal sentence ending |
| *{t'hy'la}* | t'hy'la (no honorifics at all) |

**Grammar.**

- **Raise the other person** with *-{honorific}* on the verb or with *{you-honored}*.
- **Lower yourself** with *-{humble}*, and call yourself *{nash-ve}* ("this one").
- **Between t'hy'la, use none of it.**

**Examples**

- *{vocative} {mentor}, {request} {tu} {speak}-{honorific}.* "Respected teacher, please speak."
- *{nash-ve} {not} {know}-{humble}.* "This one does not know."
- *{tu} {t'hy'la} {my}.* "You are my t'hy'la." (Plain speech is itself the honor.)

**Practice.** (1) Respected healer, please come. (2) This one does not understand. (3) Make (1) formal.

**Answers.** (1) *{vocative} {healer}, {request} {come}.* (2) *{nash-ve} {not} {understand}-{humble}.* (3) *{vocative} {healer}, {request} {come} {formal}.*

## Lesson 8: Feeling and touch, the Vulcan way

**Words**

| Vulcan | English |
|---|---|
| *{anger}*, *{fear}*, *{grief}*, *{joy}* | anger, fear, grief, joy |
| *{noting}* | noted (closes an emotion statement) |
| *{ev-melded}* | known by touch |
| *{touch-hum}* | touch, asking leave |
| *{grant-leave}* | you have leave |
| *{forgive-display}* | forgive the display |

**Grammar.**

- **A Standard Vulcan does not say "I am afraid."** They say "fear is in me. Noted."
- **They do not state someone else's feeling** without an evidential.
- **They ask leave before any touch.**

**Examples**

- *{fear} {in} {nash-ve} {noting}.* "There is fear in me. Noted."
- *{ish-ve} {grief} {ev-inferred}.* "She is grieving, I conclude."
- *{request} {nash-ve} {touch-hum}?* "May this one touch you?" Reply: *{grant-leave}.*
- *{request} {forgive-display}.* "Please forgive the display."

**Romulan contrast:** a Romulan states feeling freely and marks rank instead. *Mnek'nra* is "good," said up to a superior.

**Practice.** (1) There is joy in me. Noted. (2) He is angry, I conclude. (3) May this one touch you?

**Answers.** (1) *{joy} {in} {nash-ve} {noting}.* (2) *{ish-ve} {anger} {ev-inferred}.* (3) *{request} {nash-ve} {touch-hum}?*
