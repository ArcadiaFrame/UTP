# Word-building

Vulcan has a small stock of old roots and builds everything else from them. A student is a "learning one" (*{student}*), a school a "learning place" (*{school}*), and the Federation is "worlds joined" (*{federation}*), on the pattern of canon *kash-kau*, "minds joined." @STATS@

## Where the rules come from

1. **Canon Vulcan is the base.** Every process below is modeled on something canon shows:
   - the bound verb *-tor*, "do, make," in *dif-tor*, "live long"
   - the bound noun *ve*, "one, person," in *ish-ve* and *nash-ve*
   - *kau*, "joined," in *kash-kau*
   - the participle *-ik* in *seheik*, "declared"
   - the diminutive *-kam*
   - the old compounds *plak tow* (blood fever), *pon far* (time of fire) and *koon-ut* (bond rite)
2. **Anthropology and evolutionary linguistics decide what is old.** The meanings every human society names first are roots, and so are the Vulcan ones: body, kin, desert and sky, motion, eating, the basic emotions, small numbers, and the basic qualities (these are the meanings on the Swadesh and Leipzig-Jakarta lists). Meanings that come with social complexity, institutions, abstraction and technology are built later from older words. Colour terms arrive in the Berlin-Kay order.
3. **English, Latin and Japanese cross-check.** Where two of the three build a meaning from another word, Vulcan does too, but with its own pieces. Latin *veri-ficare*, "make true," sits behind Vulcan *{verify}*.

## The endings

|---|---|---|---|---|

| *-{indef}* | "some-" on a question word | Classical | Japanese *dare-ka*, Latin *ali-quis* | *{someone}* someone, *{sometimes}* sometimes |
| *-{univ}* | "every-" on a question word | Classical | Japanese *dare-mo*, Latin *quis-que* | *{everyone}* everyone, *{everywhere}* everywhere |

**Prefixes grew out of prepositions**, the way canon *fi'* "on, toward" became the ending *-fi*:

- *{without}-* "without, un-": *{impossible}* impossible, *{infinity}* infinity, *{atom}* atom ("uncuttable")
- *{again}-* "again, re-": *{return}* return, *{recognize}* recognize ("know again")
- *{before}-* "before, pre-": *{predict}* predict ("say before"), *{prepare}* prepare
- *{in}-* "in": *{include}* include, *{insight}* insight
- *{outside}-* "out": *{express}* express ("push out")
- *{with}-* "with": *{compassion}* compassion ("suffering with")

**Fields of study** follow the pattern *{inquiry} t'X*, "inquiry of X," as in *{science}* science. For example *{astronomy}* (astronomy, "inquiry of stars"), *{biology}* (biology) and *{history}* (history, "inquiry of the past").

**Two roots joined** make compounds, the oldest process of all: *{capital}* capital ("head-city"), *{east}* east ("sun-rise"), *{mirage}* mirage ("false water"), *{firepot}* firepot (canon *far*, "fire," + pot).

## Order of the pieces

The order is fixed, as in Japanese. Prefix, then root, then building endings, then honorific or humble, then *-{ongoing}* (ongoing), then *-{past-un}* (past). *-{object-wizh}* goes on the last word of the object phrase, and the evidential comes last in the sentence.

*{return}-{ongoing}-{past-un}*, "was returning" · *{student}-{plural}-{object-wizh}*, "the students" (as object)

## The era rule

A built word can never be older than its pieces; the checker refuses one that is (E11). So "green," *{green}*, built on canon *plak* "blood," is a Classical word, not a Tribal one. Quality nouns in *-{abstract}* become productive in the Classical era, and Surak's philosophers multiplied them.

## Where Vulcan culture overrides

- **Green is blood-coloured** (*{green}*), because Vulcan blood is green.
- **Blue is void-coloured** (*{blue}*). Vulcan's sky is pale and red, so blue is rare and late, the colour of the deep sky at dusk.
- **A mirage is "false water"** (*{mirage}*), a desert people's word.
- **Privacy is "the self kept to itself"** (*{privacy}*): self + adjective + quality.
- **The arts are Ways**, as in Japanese: calligraphy is *{calligraphy}*, "the way of writing."

## Making a new word

1. Search the dictionary first.
2. If a basic meaning is missing (a body part, a desert animal), it gets a new root from the generator.
3. Otherwise build it from existing pieces with the endings above, keeping the era rule.
4. Add it to `lexicon/meanings.csv` (a root) or `tools/derivation.py` (a built word), then run the build. The checker refuses duplicates and sound-alikes.

## Numbers and kin

**Numbers are regular**, as in Japanese: ten-one is eleven and two-ten is twenty. A number follows its noun, as other modifiers do.

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 *{one}* | 10 *{ten}* | 11 *{eleven}* | 20 *{twenty}* |
| 12 *{twelve}* | 19 *{nineteen}* | 30 *{thirty}* | 90 *{ninety}* |
| 100 *{hundred}* | 1,000 *{thousand}* | first *{first}* | second *{second-o}* |
| a third *{third-part}* | a quarter *{quarter}* | | |

**Kin terms are descriptive**, as in clan societies. An aunt or uncle is "parent-sibling," *{aunt-uncle}*, as Swedish says *farbror*, "father-brother." A niece or nephew is "sibling-child," *{niece-nephew}*. A cousin is a "clan-sibling," *{cousin}*: in a society of great Houses, cousins grow up as siblings, as in the Hawaiian and Iroquois kin systems. Elder and younger siblings keep their own old roots (*{elder-sibling}*, *{younger-sibling}*), because seniority matters.
