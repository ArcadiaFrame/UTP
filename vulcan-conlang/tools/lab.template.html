Sound laws

Laws run top to bottom on each word. A pattern is a regular expression on the romanized form: ^ marks the start of a word, $ the end, and $1 in a replacement repeats the first bracketed group. Test cases rerun as you type.

Edits stay in this browser only. To make a change permanent, copy the laws below and paste them into chat; they go into tools/rules.json, which the scripts and this page both build from.

Dictionary

Every root is reconstructed in Proto-Vulcan and carried into each branch by the sound laws. Canon words are anchors the laws must reproduce; the rest come from the generator, and none were accepted until they passed every check against the words already in.

Glosser

Pick Vulcan or Romulan, then type a sentence in standard spelling and see it split into words and endings, each with its meaning. Hyphens before endings are optional. Canon spellings like sahrafel are tidied to standard spelling first. Anything not in the dictionary is flagged, which is how gaps get found.

Phrasebook

Everyday lines for conversation. Click one to gloss it above. The check below runs every line through the Glosser, so a phrase that uses a missing word shows up at once.

Word order is Subject, then Object, then Verb, with the evidential last. Endings attach to the end of a word: -wizh marks the object, -un the past, -fi direction toward. See the Grammar tab in the design bible for the full sketch.

Corpus

Every attested form the reconstruction must explain. Canon is fixed evidence; novel and Romulan forms guide choices. Maker layer records who wrote the line, which is how inconsistencies become eras and dialects.

Words that don't translate fully

Some Vulcan words bundle ideas that English keeps apart, or carry feeling and sacred weight no English word holds. Every language has these, like je ne sais quoi or saudade. Each card gives the word's full range, why English falls short, and the closest Earth parallels a translator can lean on.
