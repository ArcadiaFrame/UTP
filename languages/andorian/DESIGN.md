## Decisions

### Who speaks it, where, and what do they talk about most?

**Clan people of the ice, with an Imperial Guard.** Canon shows Andorians in cities under the ice of Andoria, organised by clans (clan names close every formal name), with a standing military and a code of honour (the ushaan). Everyday talk is clan, debt, honour, ice and war; the Aenar are the same people long separated, speaking their own branch.

Why: Arcadia's choice, with the musicians option as the deepest layer: the oldest roots (Clan era) are song, pitch and vibration.

Sources: S1.2, S1.3, S1.4, S3.2, S4.3

Considered and set aside: Musicians first, soldiers later; Bond and family first.

### How do their bodies make and hear language? Any extra channel?

**Antennae carry stance, as grammar.** The antennae move with emotion on screen. Languages turn a channel their speakers always use into grammar (tone, evidentials). Andorian marks the speaker's stance with a small set of obligatory antenna positions that pair with spoken particles, so a sentence can be said in words alone (to aliens, or in the dark) and the particle stands in for the antennae.

Why: Arcadia's choice: stance is grammar, with a spoken particle standing in when the antennae cannot be seen.

Sources: S1.3, S4.1

Considered and set aside: Antennae are body language only; Antennae hear: pitch and vibration matter.

### What about their world must the vocabulary be rich in?

**Both, ranked: ice first, the giant for time and myth.** Ice and the underground for everyday words; the giant in the sky for time, calendar, myth and the ceremonial register.

Why: Arcadia's choice.

Sources: S1.2, S4.2

Considered and set aside: Ice and the underground; The sky over a gas giant.

### What split the speakers into branches, and when?

**Aenar kept the old language.** A small, isolated, pacifist people preserves the old forms (as Icelandic preserved Old Norse), while Andorii changed through empire, war and contact. Aenar words then sound archaic and ceremonial to Andorians, which suits the Code of the Ushaan and ritual.

Why: Arcadia's choice: most sound changes fall on Andorii; Aenar forms read as archaic and ceremonial.

Sources: S1.2, S1.3

Considered and set aside: Aenar diverged in isolation, and innovated; Many clan dialects, and a second language.

### What should it sound like, given the attested words?

**Fricative-rich, with plain stops.** Roots favour th, sh, zh, ch and r-clusters, but plain stops (p, t, k, d, g) and doubled consonants are native too, so every canon form fits: thaan and zhavey as well as pednog and kayenn. Sayable at the table on the first try.

Why: Arcadia's choice.

Sources: S1.1, S3.1, S3.2

Considered and set aside: Hissing and breathy; Andorii sharp, Aenar soft.

### Word order, and what the grammar must mark (evidence, rank, sex, number...)

**Verb-final, with a final question word.** In the one canon question, 'Arabic kayenn?' ('Is this Arabic?'), the predicate comes first and kayenn closes the sentence: a sentence-final question word, typical of verb-final languages. Verb-final (SOV) with postpositions; the grammar marks stance (from the antennae, if chosen), the four sexes in pronouns, and an honour register.

Why: Arcadia's choice.

Sources: S1.1

Considered and set aside: Verb-first, for a commanding sound; Free order with case endings.

### Pronouns, sexes and genders, kinship terms

**Four-way throughout.** Four third-person pronouns by sex, the four attested parent words (zhavey, shreya, thavan, charan), sex prefixes in names (zh', sh', th', ch'), and polite address by sex (Zha, Sha, Cha, Tha). With other species Andorians accept he or she as an analogue, which implies their own language marks four.

Why: Arcadia's choice.

Sources: S3.1, S3.2, S2.1, S4.2

Considered and set aside: Four-way in names and kinship only; Canon only: two sexes, marriages of four.
