# Real-world parallels for an Andorian language (science)

Notes in the compiler's own words, November 2026, for plausibility only. They never override canon.

## Antennae as an extra channel

In insects, antennae carry Johnston's organ, which detects vibration and near-field sound; mosquitoes and fruit flies hear through their antennae. A species that hears or feels through antennae could carry part of its communication in antenna position and movement, the way human speech carries meaning in tone, gesture and facial expression. Spoken languages often turn a channel the speakers always use into grammar: tone languages use pitch for word meaning, and many languages mark the speaker's evidence or stance with a small set of obligatory particles (evidentials). Sign and speech can work together in one language: Plains Indian Sign Language was used alongside many spoken languages.

## Four-way social categories

Australian section systems (for example Kariera) divide a society into four named sections; a person's section decides whom they may marry and what their children are called, and kinship terms follow the sections. Grammatical gender in the world's languages ranges from none to many: Bantu languages have more than ten noun classes, Dyirbal four. A language with four sexes could have four third-person pronouns, four sets of kinship terms, and agreement by sex, or it could mark sex only in names and kinship, as many languages mark gender only in pronouns.

## Cold, ice and underground living

Polar peoples have rich vocabularies for ice, snow and travel conditions, although the claim that Inuit has hundreds of words for snow is overstated: much of the richness comes from word-building, not separate roots. Underground and cave-dwelling life favours words for darkness, echo, warmth and the routes between chambers. Cold air carries sound well over ice, and low-frequency sound travels far.

## Honour cultures

Honour-based societies (in anthropology, cultures where reputation must be defended personally) tend to have fine distinctions for insult, debt, vengeance and formal challenge, and formal registers for addressing rank.
